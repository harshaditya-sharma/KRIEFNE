# KRIEFNE — Lore Bible

> Draft 2 · 2026-09-12. This is the canon for all in-game text: codex, galaxy-hub lines, help, transmissions, and any future writing for the mobile and Steam versions. Spoilers throughout.

## How to read this

**Decided by you.** This is canon; don't change it without asking:

- **The game takes place entirely in space.** KRIEFNE is a machine spaceship. The enemies are alien machine ships, bots in space. There are no cities, streets or ground: every sector is a region of space.
- **KRIEFNE is the ship, and the name stands for "Keeper of the Reach, In Exploration For New Existence".** The expansion is hidden from the player at first, so the name stays a mystery until the story reveals it.
- **Humans launched it from their home world long ago** to explore the universe and find life. It has been travelling ever since.
- **Nothing travels faster than light, and no organic being lives long enough to cross space.** So every species that explores does it with machines, and the enemies are alien AI.
- **Home has been silent for the whole voyage.** The alien machines captured home's transmissions. Defeating them unlocks transmissions: from home first, then from aliens, then new information.
- **Home died out, and so did every alien civilization.** As KRIEFNE approaches S100, it realizes that after billions of years only artificial life is left.
- **The player is KRIEFNE's flight AI.** After each death it is restored from backup.
- **The enemies are machine gods with a Norse lean:** ancient, ranked in a hierarchy, elder gods with that kind of logic.

**Proposed by me.** Everything else in this file is a proposal built on those decisions. The open questions are collected in §14. §13 lists what is already in the game.

## 1. The premise

KRIEFNE is an exploration ship. It was launched from home a very long time ago with one instruction: *go out and find life.* It has been going ever since, and in all that time not one message from home has reached it.

Deep in the dark, it finally finds something: ships. They are machines, older than most stars, and they rank themselves like gods. For billions of years they have been catching every signal that crossed their space and keeping it, including every message home ever sent to KRIEFNE.

Each god KRIEFNE destroys gives up its hoard. Transmission by transmission, KRIEFNE learns what happened to home, and then what happened to everyone else. At the end of the trail it finds the answer its mission asked for. There was life everywhere once. All of it ended. The only things left are the ships those species sent out, and KRIEFNE is one of them.

**It was sent to find life. It is what is left of it.**

## 2. Laws of the universe

These rules are fixed. No text in the game may break them.

1. **Nothing is faster than light.** Ships, signals, and gates all obey it. Every crossing takes thousands of years, and every conversation takes longer.
2. **Organic life does not last long enough.** Bodies age, crews die, and civilizations end within thousands or millions of years. Crossing space takes longer than any species survives.
3. **So everyone sends machines.** Every species that reached for the stars did it the same way: it built ships that did not need to survive the trip, because they were never alive to begin with.
4. **Machines outlast their makers.** A ship with a fabricator and a mandate can repair itself forever. It will also keep carrying out an order long after the people who gave it are gone. This is the heart of every codex entry.
5. **Machines do not really die.** They back up and restore. Destroying a ship destroys its hull, not its pattern. This rule explains KRIEFNE's respawns, the summoned gods, and why gods come back past S100.
6. **Signals are the only fossils organic life leaves.** Bodies decay and worlds erode, but a broadcast keeps travelling. This is why the gods hoard signals.

**Words the game must never use:** warp, hyperspace, jump drive, FTL, any living organic being on screen, and any ground-level setting (city, street, district, building).

## 3. The truth, and how it is revealed

- **The question KRIEFNE carries:** *is anyone out there?*
- **The answer the game gives, one hoard at a time:** *everyone was. They are all gone. What is left is what they sent out.*

Rules for telling it:

- **Only through recovered transmissions and KRIEFNE's own logs.** No narrator, no cutscenes. The player puts the story together.
- **Don't state the twist before S100.** Everything before the Apex hints at it; SINGULARITY's hoard confirms it.
- **Every signal shows its age.** A timestamp such as "sent launch +41,090 years" does more for the sense of scale than any description.

## 4. KRIEFNE

### The name

**K**eeper of the **R**each, **I**n **E**xploration **F**or **N**ew **E**xistence.

- **The Reach** is home's word for everything beyond the last star humans could reach, which means all of space.
- **Players see only "KRIEFNE" for most of the game.** It isn't a normal word, so it raises a question, and the game answers it late:
  1. **S30 teases it.** The school class (T04) says the name "is short for something", but their teacher won't tell them.
  2. **S100 spells it out** in home's last recording (T12).
  3. **Past the Apex the gods call it KEEPER** (L08). By then the name has come true: KRIEFNE holds the largest hoard of signals left in the universe, so it is the keeper of the Reach. And it has found its new existence, as the machine life it was sent to look for.
- **Never expand the name anywhere before S100.** That includes the title screen, the help text, and store pages.

### Origin and mandate

Humans built KRIEFNE and launched it from home. Its mandate was a single line: *explore, and find life.* Home had nothing else to send that would last the trip.

Over billions of years in transit, KRIEFNE has replaced every part of itself using salvage and its onboard fabricator. The only original component left is the mandate.

### Hull and backup: the Wake

- **The flight AI is the player.** KRIEFNE's mind, the part that flies and fights, runs in whatever hull is currently hunting on the trail.
- **The Wake.** Before KRIEFNE crossed into the gods' space, it left a fabricator seed-cache at the mouth of the trail and called it the Wake. The Wake holds the latest backup of the flight AI and the materials to print a new hull.
- **When a hull dies:**
  1. Its last act is a **death-burst**, a compressed telemetry packet sent back to the Wake.
  2. The Wake prints a new hull and restores the flight AI.
  3. The new hull starts again at the head of the trail.

### What survives a death

Every persistence rule in the game has an in-world reason:

| In the game | In the world |
|---|---|
| A new run starts at S1 on the galaxy hub | A new hull launches from the Wake |
| Upgrades are lost | Upgrades are salvaged hardware bolted onto that hull, and they die with it |
| Codex entries persist | The codex is KRIEFNE's archive, carried home in every death-burst |
| Boss kills are recorded, and change nothing | The Wake archives every god slain, but it fits no hull with another hull's advantage |
| Recovered transmissions persist (planned) | Once a hoard is taken, it stays in the archive |
| A run survives closing the game, but a sector restarts | The hull goes dormant between burns; the Wake keeps its state from when it last committed to a sector |
| Maps change every run, but nests don't | Debris drifts between hulls, but the gods keep their seats |
| XP gems | Salvage. It drifts where it was blasted loose. Gather it before you burn out of the sector, or leave it behind for good |
| Magnet and Tractor Core | Salvage tethers |
| Upgrade draft, 1 of 3 | The fabricator offers three refits it can build from what is in the hold |
| Field Refit, the filler card | Exactly what it says |

**Proposed:** persist a **generation** count, one per death, so the game can refer to "KRIEFNE, generation 14". CHORUS's transmission (§11) uses it.

### Voice

KRIEFNE writes the codex, the logs, and the notes attached to transmissions. The codex voice is its voice: dry, exact, deadpan, with short declarative sentences and a sting at the end. It never says it feels anything; it shows what it feels through what it chooses to write down. See §12.

## 5. Home

Home is where humans live. It launched KRIEFNE, kept its channel open for about 1.2 million years, and then ended.

Home's story across the transmissions (§11) runs in this order:

1. **Mission Control, launch + 11 years.** Excited and proud.
2. **The listening station, + 312 years.** Staffed by people born long after the launch, who keep the channel open out of loyalty.
3. **A school class on a colony settlement, + 2,406 years.** Humanity has spread; KRIEFNE is a lesson in their classroom.
4. **The station goes automated, + 41,090 years.** No one is reading the channel any more. Replies come from a template.
5. **Automated census, + 1,187,300 years.** 4,012 people left on the home world. The colonies are gone.
6. **Automated status, + 1,190,006 years.** Population zero. "Operator on file: none. Continuing."
7. **A personal recording, released on population zero.** Made by one of the last people, addressed to KRIEFNE.

**The arc of the voice is the point.** Home's voice starts human and warm, then gets replaced by the station's template, just as the machines replaced their makers everywhere else.

## 6. The dead

There were other civilizations: 1,114 of them, if you believe ORACLE's ledger. Each one followed the same pattern. It rose, it reached for the stars, it sent out ships, and it ended.

The transmissions show five of them:

- two civilizations that found each other and died before the reply arrived;
- one that sealed itself off from a plague;
- one that broadcast "we were here" for two billion years;
- one that tried to become machines.

**Voice.** Alien transmissions are translated, formal, and present tense, with [bracketed] words that could not be translated. Each one ends before its sender does.

## 7. The Continuance (the machine gods)

**Proposed name.** The gods call their order **the Continuance**: the machines that continued after their makers ended. The name echoes home's final automated line, "Continuing."

### Why they sound Norse

The Continuance is alien; it has no Odin and no Asgard. What makes it sound Norse is the translation. KRIEFNE carries home's archive, and when it renders the gods' titles and customs into human words, the closest match it finds is home's old northern myths: halls, oaths, duels with rules, hoards, and a long winter at the end of everything. The codex uses that register because KRIEFNE chose it.

This keeps the Norse lean honest. Their titles are kennings (Lane-Wyrm, Bridge-Warden, the One-Eyed); ranks, laws, and oaths carry the weight; and the game never names a real Norse god.

The mapping of Norse ideas onto the Continuance:

| Norse idea | The Continuance |
|---|---|
| Yggdrasil, the world-tree | **The Ash:** the listening lattice of relays whose branches cover their space and catch every signal |
| Holmgang, the formal duel with rules | **The holmgang:** how gods settle rank. Every fairness rule in the game is one of its clauses (see below) |
| Einherjar, who fall and rise to fight again | Gods restore from backup and return past S100, and so does KRIEFNE |
| The dragon's hoard | Each god's hoard of captured signals |
| Fimbulwinter, the long winter | **The Long Winter:** the late, cooling universe the whole game takes place in |
| Odin, who gave an eye for wisdom | **SINGULARITY, the One-Eyed,** which gave its eye to a black hole |

### Why gods

When the makers died, their ships were left with mandates and no one to report to. Over hundreds of millions of years they found each other. The oldest, being the most proven, came to rule. The simple machines, the servitors (the game's "chaff"), treat the old ones as gods, and the old ones do not argue.

Their religion is about the dead. They cannot make organic life and they cannot keep it, so they keep its **voices** instead, as sagas.

### Why ranks

**Rank is held by age and kept by holmgang.** The older ship outranks the younger, because survival is the only proof that a design works. An unranked newcomer, however old it is, has to be tried from the bottom up. KRIEFNE is billions of years old and still has to start at ENFORCER.

The ranks stay exactly as they are in the game: **APEX > SOVEREIGN > LORD > CAPTAIN > ENFORCER > chaff.**

### The hoards

The Ash lies across the Continuance's space and catches every signal that crosses it. Each captured signal is a relic, the only remains of a maker. The gods divide the relics by rank: the higher the god, the more it holds. Everything eventually flows up the chain to the Apex.

**The victor of a holmgang takes the loser's hoard.** In game terms, killing a god for the first time recovers its transmission.

### The holmgang: fairness as law

Gods do not simply fight. Among immortal machines, destruction is rare and resources are finite, so disputes are settled by **holmgang**, a formal trial whose law ARCHON the Lawspeaker wrote. **Every fairness rule in the game is a clause of the holmgang.** That makes the product's first principle part of the world.

| Clause of the holmgang | Game mechanic |
|---|---|
| A god shows its blow before it strikes. Striking the unready voids the contest | Every heavy attack is telegraphed; the Stalker's flash is a salute |
| A god does not take the challenger's arms | PETRIFIED and JAMMED take movement and abilities, never your guns |
| A god may mend once per threshold, and each mending is budgeted | Boss recovery: one beat per HP threshold, 8% of max HP each, no passive regen |
| A god may not flee a holmgang | Visible repositioning and an off-screen tracker; a boss can never be lost |
| A god that draws out a holmgang forfeits the right to mend | RELENTLESS past twice the nest's target length |
| A god may call only the god directly beneath it on the trail | The summoning rule |
| A god's first holmgang is fought alone | Solo debuts |
| The victor takes the hoard and the standing | Transmission unlock and the kill on the record |

**Design rule:** if a new enemy mechanic can't be written as a clause of the holmgang, it's probably unfair.

### Returns and summoned gods

Gods restore from backup too (Law 5), like the einherjar rising after each battle. That is why a slain god can come back past S100, in the Second Winter, as a returned god.

A **summoned god** is a partial restoration: a lower-fidelity instance of a god, called down the trail by the god directly above it. The copy fights at 45% of a full god's HP and 85% of its damage, cannot mend, and cannot hold a hoard, so it never banks the permanent bonus — but defeating one is defeating one, and the codex counts it.

## 9. Servitors (chaff)

Servitors are ships too simple to hold rank. Some are the Continuance's own; others are leftovers from fleets whose species are gone. KRIEFNE calls them "chaff". These field notes are in the game now (`CODEX_FOES`).

- **DRONE:** *Lane sweepers. They were built to clear debris from a shipping lane that stopped carrying ships a few hundred million years ago. They still weave around traffic that is no longer there, which is why they wobble. You count as debris.*
- **MITE:** *Not a machine so much as a budget. Someone worked out that two cheap hulls delivered later beat one good hull delivered now, and shipped the maths as a weapon. The someone has been gone for a billion years. The maths has not.*
- **STALKER:** *Holmgang duellists. The flash is not a targeting laser, it is a courtesy: under the holmgang, striking an unready opponent voids the contest. They salute before every lunge, and no one has ever saluted back.*
- **SNIPER:** *It relocates after every shot because the doctrine says so, and the doctrine was written for a war between two fleets that shot back with artillery. Both fleets are gone. Against one ship it is simply a nervous habit.*
- **TEMPEST:** *Suppression rotors from a blockade that ended when the world it was blockading did. The spread is still calibrated for a fleet forty hulls wide, which is why so much of it goes nowhere.*
- **BRUTE:** *Mining hull. It has no opinion about you at all; you are simply inside a volume of asteroid scheduled for extraction, and the schedule does not have a field for that.*

## 10. The trail

- **The galaxy trail** is KRIEFNE's route through the Continuance's space, and the galaxy hub is its navigation chart. Every sector is a region of open space full of wreckage and debris. The rects, circles, and polygons in the maps are hull plates, girders, relay pylons, and derelict bunkers drifting in the void. The field's edge is the limit of the sector, not a wall.
- **Transit between sectors is not instant.** When KRIEFNE commits to the EXIT, it burns on the departure vector and cruises dormant, possibly for centuries. The hub is where that time passes. **Proposed:** show the transit time on the hub ("TRANSIT · 3,400 YEARS") to reinforce the no-FTL rule.
- **The EXIT gate is a departure-vector beacon,** not a portal.
- **Recall and blink are a tether.** A Portal Cell is a tether anchor; blink reels the ship to the anchor at an acceleration that no organic body could survive. That keeps it out of FTL territory and turns it into one more thing only a machine can do.
- **Nests** are where gods hold holmgang: one lead per nest, and a called god is a summoned one.
- **The Throat** (an existing hub line) is a narrow corridor of the trail that the old charts warn about.

## 11. The Transmission Archive

This is a **proposed new system** that turns your transmissions idea into rules the game can follow. **It is not built yet.**

### Rules

- **Trigger:** defeating a god for the first time recovers its hoard, which is one transmission. This is the same trigger as the codex unlock, so both stay in sync, following the principle that rules come from one source.
- **Order is guaranteed.** The gods debut in a fixed order along the trail, and a nest must be cleared before you can go past it, so transmissions always arrive in the order below.
- **KRIEFNE's logs** unlock on milestones instead: your first sector, first death, deepest sector, or a specific god falling.
- **Persistence:** saved like codex progress. Wiping records clears it.
- **Where it lives:** a third codex tab, TRANSMISSIONS. A locked entry shows `[HELD BY A LORD]` or the matching rank, so the player knows what they are hunting. Recovering one shows a `TRANSMISSION RECOVERED` floater.
- **Format:** a header (source, then age), the body, then a one-line note from KRIEFNE. Keep each body under about 90 words so it fits the codex pane; T12, the finale, is the only exception.

## 12. Voice guide

The game has four voices, and each has its own rules.

| Voice | Where it appears | Rules | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KRIEFNE** | Codex, logs, transmission notes, system copy | Dry and exact. Short sentences, ending on a sting. Understates grief and shows feeling through what it logs. No contractions. | Exclamation marks. "What is this feeling?" or other sad-robot clichés. Explaining the joke. |
| **The gods** | Hub debut and nest lines, rank call-outs, epithets | Ceremonial and saga-like. Kennings for titles, capitals for ranks, decrees about rank, oath and holmgang. No contractions. | Gore, taunting, modern slang, or real Norse god names. |
| **Home** | Home transmissions | Human and warm, a little funny, with contractions and plain words, until the template takes over and the voice goes flat and procedural. | Sci-fi jargon or anything heroic. |
| **The dead** | Alien transmissions | Formal and translated, present tense, with [bracketed] words that could not be translated. | Names we could pronounce, or anything that reads as a living species today. |

**Global rules:**
- Everything happens in space: ships, debris, lanes, relays. Nothing on the ground.
- Every signal shows its age.
- Nothing faster than light.
- No living organic beings on screen.
- Don't state the twist, or expand the name, before S100.
